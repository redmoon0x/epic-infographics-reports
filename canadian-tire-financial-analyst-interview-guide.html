1. What is this job?

This is mainly an accounts payable and invoice-checking job, with extra focus on freight and parcel charges. You help make sure the company pays the correct amount, to the correct vendor, with the right documents and approvals.

Simple example: A delivery company sends a bill for a shipment. You compare the bill with the agreed rate, shipment information, delivery proof, and supporting documents. If something does not match, you investigate, document the difference, and resolve it or send it to the right person.

What “Non-Audit” means here

The title says Non-Audit. The listed work is day-to-day transaction checking, invoice validation, reconciliations, and process controls—not an external-auditor role.

2. What you may do during the day

Check and process invoices

  • Review, validate, and process vendor invoices.
  • Check purchase orders, receipts, invoice details, account codes, and approvals.
  • Track exceptions and invoice status; resolve mismatches before payment.
  • Look for duplicate, overpaid, underpaid, or incorrectly coded charges.

Audit freight and parcel bills

  • Compare freight invoices with shipment details and delivery information.
  • Check agreed rates, contracts, extra/accessorial charges, and proof documents.
  • Investigate the reason for a difference and keep evidence of the decision.

Reconcile vendor records

  • Compare vendor statements with internal AP records and payment information.
  • Investigate missing invoices, unapplied credits, duplicate entries, or other differences.
  • Check proof-of-delivery records, shipment completion, delivery status, and quantities.

Support claims, returns, and shop supplies

  • Check shop-supplies bills against the right store, cost centre, or accounting code.
  • Help process damaged-goods claims using claims, product, and supporting details.
  • Validate returned-goods debit bills, quantities, pricing, approvals, and documentation.

Meet service targets and improve quality

  • Monitor accuracy, turnaround time, backlog, and productivity against KPIs/SLAs.
  • Spot repeated errors, process gaps, control weaknesses, or automation opportunities.
  • Follow service-level agreements and record required information in the right systems.

Work with the wider team

  • Communicate with client contacts, vendors, and internal/global teams.
  • Help with training, onboarding, process documents, testing, and updated workflows.
  • Escalate high-risk or unresolved issues with facts, evidence, and a suggested next step.

3. What to prepare before the interview

Accounting and accounts payable

  • What an invoice, purchase order (PO), goods receipt, vendor statement, and credit note are.
  • How to compare an invoice with its PO and receipt (often called a three-way match).
  • How to handle a mismatch in amount, quantity, date, vendor, tax, or account code.
  • Why approvals, supporting documents, and duplicate checks matter.
  • How vendor reconciliation works and how you would investigate an unmatched item.

Freight audit

  • How to compare the freight bill with the contract/agreed rate and shipment details.
  • What extra/accessorial charges might need separate evidence or approval.
  • How you would detect duplicate billing or an incorrect delivery/shipment charge.
  • How you would document a discrepancy and follow through to resolution.

Excel and systems

  • Be ready to describe your actual Excel level and how you check lists of transactions.
  • If you know them, revise filters, sorting, basic formulas, lookups, and pivot tables.
  • Explain how you learn a new ERP or accounting system carefully and follow controls.

These are sensible areas to revise; the screenshots do not specify an Excel test or particular software.

Prepare real examples

  • A time you found or corrected an error.
  • A time you handled a mismatch, deadline, or high-volume task.
  • A time you communicated with a customer/vendor/team member to resolve an issue.
  • A time you improved a process or prevented an error from repeating.
Use real experience. If you haven’t worked in freight audit or a particular ERP, say so plainly. Then explain the relevant skill you do have and how you would learn the process.

4. Common interview questions for this kind of role

These are likely practice questions based on the job duties—not a confirmed list from the employer.

1. Tell me about yourself.
Give a short summary of your education or work background, your relevant accounting/AP, Excel, reconciliation, or customer-service strengths, and why this role interests you.
Keep it to about 45–60 seconds. Connect your experience to accuracy, follow-up, and learning processes.
2. What do you understand about this Financial Analyst role?
“From the job description, this role supports accounts payable and invoice controls, especially freight and parcel transactions. I would validate invoices against shipment, PO, receipt, contract, and approval information; investigate differences; reconcile vendor records; and meet accuracy and turnaround targets.”
3. How would you check an invoice before processing it?
Confirm the vendor and invoice details, check for duplicates, compare the amount/quantity with the PO and receipt, verify the correct account or cost centre and approvals, review supporting documents, and record or escalate any mismatch before processing.
4. What would you do if an invoice does not match the purchase order?
Do not approve it automatically. Identify the exact difference, compare the PO, receipt, invoice, and approval documents, check whether there is an authorised change, contact the appropriate person/vendor if needed, document the finding, and resolve or escalate it according to process.
5. How would you investigate a freight invoice that is higher than the agreed rate?
Check the shipment and contract/rate details, separate the base charge from extra charges, verify supporting evidence and approvals for each extra fee, and compare with the invoice. Document the variance and request correction or escalate it if it is not supported.
6. How do you identify a duplicate invoice?
Compare key fields such as vendor, invoice number, date, amount, PO, and shipment/reference number. Check for minor formatting differences or repeat submissions, review payment status, and follow the control process before taking action.
7. What is accounts payable?
Accounts payable is the money a business owes suppliers for goods or services already received. The AP process checks that a bill is valid, properly approved, correctly recorded, and paid on time.
8. What is a three-way match?
It is a check that compares the purchase order, receipt of the goods/services, and supplier invoice. The goal is to make sure the business ordered it, received it, and was billed correctly before payment.
9. What is reconciliation, and how would you do it?
Reconciliation means comparing two sets of records to confirm they agree. I would compare vendor statement lines with internal invoices, credits, and payments; list unmatched items; check dates and supporting records; resolve or escalate each difference; and keep a clear record of the outcome.
10. What would you do if a vendor statement shows an invoice that is missing from your records?
Ask for or locate the invoice and supporting documents, check whether it was received under another reference or is pending/duplicated, confirm goods/services and approvals, then record and process it correctly or explain/escalate why it cannot be processed.
11. How do you maintain accuracy when processing many transactions?
Use a consistent checklist, follow the documented process, verify key fields, avoid rushing through exceptions, use system controls and spreadsheet checks appropriately, and review unusual items. I would track work so that accuracy and deadlines are both visible.
12. How do you manage deadlines and a growing backlog?
Check priority, due dates, value/risk, and service commitments; organise the work; communicate early if a deadline is at risk; and escalate blockers with a concise status and next step. I would not hide a backlog or sacrifice required checks to appear faster.
13. Tell me about a time you found an error.
Use a real example: what you noticed, how you verified it, who you informed, what action you took, and what happened. If possible, explain how you helped prevent the same error from happening again.
14. What would you do if a vendor disagreed with your finding?
Stay professional and fact-based. Recheck the records, explain the specific difference and the documents used, ask for any missing evidence, document the conversation, and involve the right stakeholder if it remains unresolved.
15. When would you escalate an issue?
When the issue is high-risk or high-value, remains unresolved after the normal checks, involves missing/unclear approvals or suspected duplicate/fraudulent activity, threatens a deadline or service commitment, or is outside my authority. I would share the facts, evidence, impact, and what I recommend next.
16. What Excel functions or features can you use?
Answer honestly. Name only tools you can actually use, and give a small example—such as sorting/filtering transactions, using formulas to compare totals, or using a lookup/pivot table if you know it. If you are still learning a feature, say that directly.
17. Have you used an ERP or accounting system?
Name the system only if you have used it. Explain what you did in it, such as entering invoices, checking approvals, or reviewing vendor records. If you have not used one, describe how you learn software and follow written controls.
18. How do you handle confidential financial information?
Access only what is needed for work, use approved channels and systems, verify recipients before sharing, follow company rules, and never disclose passwords, customer/vendor data, or internal information outside authorised work.
19. How do you respond when you do not know an answer?
Do not bluff. State what you do know, explain how you would verify the answer using the process or an appropriate teammate, and describe how you would follow up.
20. Why should we hire you?
Choose two or three strengths that are true for you—such as careful checking, reliable follow-up, communication, Excel, or learning new processes. Link each one to the actual work in this role and give a brief proof/example.
21. Are you comfortable working from office and with global/offshore teams?
Answer honestly. The job details state five office days in Mangalore and 11 a.m.–8 p.m. IST, and the JD mentions global/offshore coordination. Confirm only what you can genuinely commit to.
22. Do you have any questions for us?
Yes. Prepare two or three questions from the next section. Ask what you genuinely want to know; you do not need to ask all of them.

5. A simple way to structure answers

For past-experience questions: STAR

  1. Situation: What was happening?
  2. Task: What were you responsible for?
  3. Action: What did you personally do?
  4. Result: What changed or was resolved?

Keep the setup short; spend most of the answer on your actions and result.

For “what would you do?” questions

  1. Understand the issue and clarify what is missing.
  2. Compare records and check the evidence.
  3. Identify the cause and take the permitted action.
  4. Document, communicate, and follow up.
Good habit: Pause briefly before answering. Keep your response focused on the question. If something is unclear, politely ask the interviewer to repeat or explain it.
