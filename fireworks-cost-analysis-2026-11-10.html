Bottom line

  1. Fine-tuning: yes, and it is the cheapest part of the plan. A realistic 27B job — 120 million training tokens, LoRA — costs $360 once. Full-parameter training costs twice that. No separate serving fee is added.
  2. The catch is deployment, not training. Fireworks does not let a model you trained run on its shared per-token endpoint. It must sit on dedicated GPUs at $8.00 per GPU-hour, which is $5,840 a month if you never switch it off.
  3. For 27B and 70B, Fireworks has no per-token option at all. Both classes are marked Serverless: Not supported — on-demand GPUs only. The same models trade on OpenRouter at $0.10–0.15 in / $0.32–1.88 out, so renting the hardware costs 2–7 times more for a 27B and 10–40 times more for a 70B.
  4. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is the opposite case. It is 552B of mixture-of-experts, and Fireworks serves it per token at $0.30 / $1.20 (spot $0.21 / $0.84). Self-hosting it costs $4.44–8.89 per million blended tokens at full utilisation — 10 to 30 times the serverless price. It also cannot be fine-tuned at all.
  5. Verdict. Use Fireworks to fine-tune, and to serve flagship models where its price matches the market or you need data residency and an SLA. Do not use it as a cheap host for 27B or 70B models. OpenRouter is cheaper for off-the-shelf tokens; the only reasons to pay more are control, residency, latency and contractual uptime.

Blended cost = the average price of one token when prompt and answer tokens are mixed 4:1, the shape of a typical chat or agent workload. It is the only fair way to compare an input-heavy serverless price with a GPU rental bill.

01 What Fireworks actually sells

Fireworks runs open models for you on three commercial shapes, and trains models on three more. The billing unit changes with the shape, and that unit is what decides your bill.

On the serverless endpoint you also choose a mode per request, and the mode moves the price by up to 50%:

  • Standard — the default.
  • Priority — 25% more, prioritised during peak load.
  • Fast — 100+ tokens per second for interactive apps.
  • Batch — 50% off input and output for work that can wait.
  • Spot — 30% off Standard on spare capacity; throughput can fall to zero, so you need a fallback.
  • Cached input — half price or less, no write fee. Long agent runs reach cache hit rates up to 99.6%.

Region surcharges: US-only serverless on selected models carries a 50% premium; a region-restricted GPU deployment is billed at 1.5 times the standard GPU rate.

The catch nobody puts in the headline

“Neither custom base models nor LoRA addons are supported for serverless inference. All user-provided models, including trained models, require a dedicated deployment.” Fireworks documentation, Models overview

So a $360 fine-tune does not unlock a cheap per-token endpoint. It hands you a model that only runs on GPUs you rent by the second. Fireworks is explicit that the training itself adds no serving surcharge — “you pay for the deployment by the GPU-second, plus the cost of the training job” — but the deployment is the expensive half:

Two things you cannot fine-tune

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash: “Fine-tuning is not supported for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on Fireworks, and no V4.1 variant offers it.” You can only buy its tokens.

Anything you cannot name in the model library: the tunable list is model by model. Qwen3.8 27B, Qwen3.6 27B and Llama 3.3 70B all say “Fine-tuning supported”; the DeepSeek V4.1 family does not.

Free right now

Managed SFT of Muse Glimmer 30B is free through 30 November 2026, in the UI and through the Serverless Training API, with no credit card. It is a 30B model in your exact size band — the cheapest way to test whether a tuned mid-size model beats the big model you are paying for today.

Three patterns matter more than any single number:

  • On flagships, Fireworks is at the market price, not below it. Kimi K3 at $3.00 / $15.00 is exactly what Moonshot, Together and Baseten charge. Gemini-tier margins are not on offer here.
  • On DeepSeek, Fireworks sits in the middle of a very wide field. 37 endpoints serve that same model on OpenRouter, from $0.112 / $0.336 to $0.45 / $1.80 — a 4× spread for identical weights. Fireworks is roughly 2.7× the cheapest on input.
  • The mid-size models you asked about are not on Fireworks serverless at all. A 27B or a 70B there means a GPU rental, so comparing it with a per-token price is comparing a fixed cost with a variable one.
Why the same model has six prices

Quantization, uptime and rate limits. A 27B sold at $0.035 input is running 4-bit weights; the same model at 16-bit costs several times more to serve and is priced accordingly. Artificial Analysis publishes an endpoint accuracy index for exactly this reason: cheaper endpoints can lose measurable accuracy against the reference. When a price looks too good, check the quantization column before switching.

In monthly volume terms: one H100 at $8.00 per hour costs $5,840 a month, which buys 11.8 billion blended tokens of a 27B model at OpenRouter's $0.495 per million. Two H100s beat the 70B token price only above 81 billion blended tokens a month. For the 552B model the crossover is roughly 300 billion. Almost no product is there. The basis for each comparison is the cheapest 8-bit endpoint for that model, not the cheapest 4-bit one — otherwise the comparison would flatter self-hosting.

What the rental price leaves out
  • People. A serving stack needs updates, on-call, capacity planning and security patching. A fifth of an engineer is $1,200–2,000 a month, which is a third of the GPU bill on a single H100.
  • Idle time. A fixed monthly cost does not fall when traffic does. The token price does.
  • Failure. One replica is zero redundancy. Production needs two, which doubles everything above.
  • Model refresh. Every new checkpoint is a redeploy, a re-benchmark and a rollback plan. On a serverless endpoint it is a model name change.

Why can OpenRouter's sellers undercut raw rental? Scale and batch size. A provider running thousands of GPUs at 85–95% utilisation, buying capacity on long contracts, and packing every GPU with far more concurrent sequences than a single-tenant deployment can reach, gets several times the tokens per GPU-hour that you will. DeepInfra's 70B at $0.10 / $0.32 is that arithmetic, not a mistake.

05 What to do

One rule covers most of it: buy tokens for models you did not change, rent GPUs only for models you did — or where the data cannot leave.

Where to start this week

  • Take the free Muse Glimmer 30B SFT run before 30 November. It costs nothing and answers the only question that matters commercially: does a tuned 30B beat the model we currently pay $3–15 per million for, on our own data?
  • Price the tuned model against the model it replaces, not against nothing. A tuned 27B at $1.23–2.47 per 1M blended only wins if it replaces something at $2.50 or more, or cuts answer length enough to change the blended mix.
  • Keep a per-token route open. Fireworks' price on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash ($0.30 / $1.20) is triple the cheapest mixed-provider route ($0.112 / $0.336). Route by endpoint, not by vendor loyalty.
  • Never design a product that requires a 24/7 idle GPU unless the volume is already there. Scale-to-zero, batch and spot tiers exist for exactly this reason.

06 Method, assumptions and what could change the answer

Assumptions

  • Prices are public list rates in USD, checked 10 November 2026, excluding tax.
  • 730 hours per month for a 24/7 deployment.
  • Blended cost assumes 4 prompt tokens per 1 answer token. An agentic workload runs closer to 10:1, which lifts the blended figure for every option but favors prefill-heavy providers.
  • Throughput figures are engineering estimates, not vendor benchmarks: 900–1,800 blended tokens/sec for a 27B on one H100, 700–1,400 for 70B on two, 2,000–4,000 for a 552B MoE on eight H200.
  • GPU counts follow weight size at 8-bit plus cache headroom. Fireworks may assign a different validated shape; the console's deployment-shape match is the authority for your account.
  • OpenRouter adds 5.5% when you buy credits ($0.80 minimum) but does not mark up inference.

Caveats

  • One inconsistency on Fireworks' own site. The pricing page lists DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at $0.30 / $1.20, while the model page shows $0.22 / $0.66. We use the pricing-page figure for Standard and report the other as a listed variant. Confirm in the console before budgeting.
  • Cheap endpoints are often quantised. The $0.035-per-1M 27B is 4-bit. If accuracy matters, compare the endpoint, not the model name.
  • Spot and batch change the ranking. Spot is 30% off but can drop to zero throughput; batch is 50% off but not interactive.
  • Reserved capacity is quoted privately. At real volume, both Fireworks and the GPU clouds discount, and the crossover points above move.
  • This compares cost, not quality. Nothing here says which model is good enough for your task — that test has to be run on your own data.
